How it works

01

Set a weekly schedule

Per-day lock and unlock times, day-off support, a few presets to start. Loosening a rule waits out a cooldown, so a change you make at 1am can't quietly undo what you set that morning.

02

Warnings

Notifications at 30, 15, 5, 2, and 1 minutes out. The early ones are dim overlays you can click through; the last five minutes get an always-on-top timer.

03

Curfew fires

A full-screen overlay covers every display and every Space. ⌘⇥, ⌘Q, and ⌘⌥Esc stop working. If you want, it also closes your apps and shuts the machine down.

Need back in? It takes a typed reason, a five-minute cooldown, and a hold-to-confirm, so getting an override costs something. Your weekly override and extension budgets sit in the menu bar, and the This Week view shows what actually happened.

Curfew is a commitment device, not a kernel-level lock. A determined person with a terminal or a second account can get around it. It only needs to make stopping the easy default, and most nights that's enough to close the laptop.

Two prompts a day, if you want them.

Curfew can ask you a couple of questions: one when you start in the morning, one on the lockout screen at night. The idea is borrowed from Sunsama's daily planning and shutdown.

01

Morning

Your first session of the day opens with a short, skippable prompt — free text, a 1–5 rating, a quick mood, whatever you set. It takes the screen but never traps you.

02

Night

When curfew fires, the lockout screen offers a quick recap before you step away. Note what got done and what's carried over, rate the day. Skip it and the clock runs anyway.

03

Journal

Entries land in a private journal that charts your ratings over the week. Export to Markdown or JSON, or let an assistant read it back. It stays on your Mac.

The questions are yours — edit them, reorder them, or turn them off in Settings.

Hook it up to Claude.

curfew-mcp is a small MCP server bundled with the app. Add it to Claude Desktop and your assistant can see how much time is left, ask for an extension, or read back your reflections when you review the week. Anything that changes a setting needs your approval in the app; reflections are read-only and never leave your Mac.

~/.claude_desktop_config.json
{
  "mcpServers": {
    "curfew": {
      "command": "/Applications/Curfew.app/Contents/Resources/curfew-mcp"
    }
  }
}
shell
$ curfew-ctl status
phase:     warning
remaining: 14 min
locks at:  6:00 PM
warning:   T-15

There's a matching CLI, curfew-ctl, in the app bundle for shell scripts and dotfile hooks. It reads the same storage the app does, so it works whether or not the app is running.
