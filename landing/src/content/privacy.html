Operator and scope

Hypertext Studio is responsible for the personal information it processes through Curfew, its website, hosted accounts, and connected services. This policy explains local app information, hosted information, and optional sharing. Feature descriptions apply when you enable the relevant feature; Curfew is currently available for Mac, and cross-platform functionality is being developed.

Information and purposes

  • Schedules, budgets, wake settings, preferences, activity history, reflections, and license keys support your boundaries, personal history, and paid features.
  • Account identity, email, sign-in and recovery information, device public keys and revocation state, OAuth and MCP grants, encrypted-record headers and timing, routing handles, and remote control reasons and audits support sign-in, synchronization, security, and authorized device control. These are unavoidable metadata for operating a hosted account.
  • Purchase entitlements, payment and refund references, and support messages support billing, licensing, refunds, and answering requests.
  • Website requests and transport information, including IP addresses, are processed to deliver pages and protect the service. Authentication uses cookies or tokens for sign-in and security. The account service disables authentication IP tracking and uses hashed rate-limit buckets; hosting providers may process transport information separately.

The local app does not include analytics, telemetry, or crash-reporting services, and we do not sell personal information or use it for advertising. Browser enforcement does not read page content, page subresources, or general browser history.

Local and hosted information

Without optional integrations, schedules, activity, reflections, and settings stay on your devices. Calendar permission reads today’s events for display and does not write them. Notifications provide warnings and alerts. Optional Accessibility permission supports shortcut interception during lockout. Optional Chrome navigation permission observes top-level HTTP and HTTPS navigation during task enforcement.

Optional camera presence detection is off by default. If enabled, frames are analyzed on your Mac in memory to detect whether a person is present, without identifying them. Frames are not stored or sent. The yes-or-no result remains until replaced or capture stops; presence changes and camera start/stop events enter the local audit log. Turning the feature off releases the camera.

If you enable the separate coordinator status-reporting feature, you choose its HTTPS server. Curfew sends a generated device identifier, enforcement phase, time zone, schedule digest, sequence counter, and observation/transition timestamps. It does not send reflections, camera frames, or the presence verdict. The receiving server operates under its own privacy and retention arrangements.

If you enable Curfew Account synchronization, synchronized content is end-to-end encrypted. Hypertext Studio coordinates ciphertext and does not hold the content keys needed to read it. Account identity, billing, device, routing, and security metadata are not covered by that content encryption. Account recovery backup codes restore sign-in only; an enrolled device or your separate Curfew Recovery Key is needed to decrypt content.

Lifetime license verification is offline. Subscription licenses refresh through our license service on launch and periodically while active, using a refresh token to retrieve a renewed signed key.

If you enable the legacy iCloud schedule feature, settings, device registration, activity heartbeats, and lockout state are stored in your private CloudKit database in your Apple account and is subject to Apple’s service and privacy terms. Future Android distribution may offer a build without Google Play Services and a GMS build using those services to reduce notification and sync latency; this is not a statement that Android is currently released.

Recipients, assistants, and task review

We use Cloudflare for hosted website and account infrastructure and Stripe for payments. Stripe receives payment details directly; Curfew receives purchase references and entitlements. Apple or Google receive information needed when you choose their sign-in services, and Apple operates optional iCloud synchronization. Support correspondence is processed through our email service. We may disclose information when required by applicable law or needed to protect rights or security; we do not grant unrelated access to your content.

When you run local curfew-mcp, a connected assistant can read your enforcement state, schedules, extension and override budgets, current-week activity events, weekly summaries including device names and device attribution, and reflections. It can request extensions or schedule changes. Reflections are read-only through MCP. Curfew asks before write requests by default; you can change that policy in Settings. The assistant may send information to its own service, where its provider’s privacy and retention terms apply. End-to-end sync encryption does not prevent sharing you authorize through an assistant.

Remote MCP clients receive only approved scopes. The hosted service handles authorization, device routing and status, requests and reasons, and audit records. Remote unlocks are time-bounded and audited. Direct unlock is off by default and requires separate authorization. You can revoke client access.

If you enable task-scoped browser enforcement, known destinations stay on your Mac. For an unfamiliar destination, Curfew sends its normalized origin and path, the current organization and task identifiers, your justification, and any challenge answer to Docket and Athena for review. It removes URL credentials, query strings, and fragments first. Review may involve AI processing under those services’ policies. Avoid submitting secrets or sensitive information in your answers.

The Chrome extension retains an initial justification only while a live challenge is pending, to survive reload; it does not retain completed justifications or challenge answers in extension state. The native browser request queue temporarily stores review inputs, then clears justification and answer text on resolution. Stale pending requests and resolved metadata are removed during later active cleanup, as explained in the retention policy. The local audit records hostname, decision, and scope type, without paths, answers, reviewer questions, or reasons. Docket, Athena, and any connected assistant may retain information they receive under their own policies; deleting Curfew’s local data or account does not delete those separate copies. This policy does not promise that downstream review data is immediately discarded.

Retention and deletion

Local activity and reflections have a rolling 52-week retention target, applied during day rollover while Curfew runs. Browser audit segments rotate with a 90-day retention policy; the active segment remains until rotation. Settings and license keys remain until removed. See the Data Retention Policy for hosted retention cutoffs, account deletion, retained purchase references, and optional technical storage details.

You can export hosted account data before deleting your account. Successful deletion removes account-scoped encrypted records and access during the request, while disassociating purchases and pseudonymizing retained security audits. It does not erase device copies, iCloud records, assistant copies, or payment-provider records. Cancel subscriptions separately before account deletion.

Security

Curfew uses signed offline license verification, encrypted content synchronization, scoped authorization, device revocation, and additional authentication for sensitive account actions. Protect your devices, credentials, and Recovery Key. No system can guarantee absolute security. See Security for implementation details and reporting.
