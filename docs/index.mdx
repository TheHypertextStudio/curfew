Curfew is a macOS app that ends your workday on purpose. You set a weekly
schedule while you are thinking clearly. Later, Curfew warns you as the time
runs down, puts a full-screen lockout in the way when the day is over, and
optionally shuts the Mac down.

Curfew is a commitment device, not a kernel-level lock. Someone with a terminal
and enough determination can get around it. The goal is to make stopping the
easy default, not to make working impossible.

## What ships today

Curfew's first release enables the schedule, the warnings, the lockout, the
activity log, the reflection gates, the `curfew-ctl` CLI, and the `curfew-mcp`
MCP server. CloudKit multi-device sync, the WidgetKit widgets, Calendar
awareness, and the root-owned privileged helper are compiled into the binary
but disabled by a feature flag until each one passes its signed-release
validation. Nothing in this documentation describes a surface you cannot reach
unless it says otherwise.

<Note>
Curfew keeps your schedule, activity, and reflections on your Mac. A connected
MCP client can read that data and may send it to its own service, so read that
client's privacy policy before you connect it. See
[Privacy](/privacy) for what is stored and where.
</Note>
