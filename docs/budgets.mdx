---
title: Extension and override budgets
description: How many exceptions you get each week, and when they come back.
---

Curfew gives you two kinds of exception and rations both. Extensions push
tonight's lock time back before it arrives. Overrides get you back in after the
lockout has already started. Each has its own weekly limit, its own duration,
and its own friction.

## The defaults

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `extensionWeeklyLimit` | 3 | Extension requests allowed per week |
| `extensionDurationMinutes` | 15 | Minutes added per extension |
| `overrideWeeklyLimit` | 2 | Overrides allowed per week |
| `overrideDurationMinutes` | 30 | Minutes unlocked per override |
| `resetWeekday` | Monday | Day both budgets refill |

All five are editable in Settings. The Getting Started flow walks you through
the extension limit and the override duration on its fourth step.

## The reset day

`ExtensionBudgetTracker` holds one budget. The app keeps two instances — one
for extensions and one for overrides — and both use the same `resetWeekday`, so
your week starts on one day rather than two.

The tracker recomputes the most recent reset boundary on every tick by walking
back through the last seven days looking for the configured weekday. When that
boundary is later than the last one it recorded, `remaining` goes back to the
weekly limit. Checking every tick rather than only on use means the budget
refills at the start of the reset day even in a week where you never asked for
anything.

If the calendar arithmetic cannot find a boundary in seven days, the tracker
does nothing rather than falling back to today. Treating "could not find a
boundary" as a no-op is what stops a pathological calendar from silently
restoring your budget mid-week.

Changing a weekly limit in Settings rebuilds the tracker. The new tracker is
seeded with the old one's boundary, so editing the limit on a Wednesday does
not hand you a fresh budget on the next tick.

## What counts against a budget

The budget displays in `curfew-ctl budget` and
[`curfew_budget`](/mcp-tools#curfew-budget) count the `extension_granted` and
`override_granted` events in the activity log since the start of the current
week, and subtract those from the configured limits. A request that is denied,
or an extension button you never held long enough to fire, does not consume a
slot.

## Checking what is left

```bash
curfew-ctl budget
```

```text
Extensions:  2/3 remaining (15 min each)
Overrides:   2/2 remaining (30 min each)
Resets:      Mon
```

`--json` gives the same numbers with `used`, `remaining`, `weekly_limit`, and
`duration_minutes` for each, plus `reset_weekday`.

An assistant can read the same figures with
[`curfew_budget`](/mcp-tools#curfew-budget). Deciding whether to spend an
extension or escalate to an override is the main thing that tool is for.

## Running out

When the extension budget hits zero, `canRequestExtension` is false for the
rest of the week and the hold-to-confirm control stays inert even at T-30.
When the override budget hits zero, `OverrideRequestPolicy.canConfirm` returns
false no matter how long your justification is. Neither one produces an error
you can retry past — the week has to roll over.
