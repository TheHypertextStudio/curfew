---
title: Warnings and the countdown
description: The five escalation stages, what each one changes, and how to retune them.
---

Curfew's warning stages are separate from its enforcement phase. The phase is
coarse — `working`, `warning`, `locked`, `dayOff`. The stage is fine-grained
and keeps escalating underneath it, which is why the app can tell you that
T-30 has passed while you are still nominally working.

## The five stages

`WarningStage.stage(forMinutesRemaining:intervals:)` maps minutes left onto a
stage. With the default thresholds:

| Stage | Fires at | Dim overlay | Floating timer | Snooze | Extension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-30 | 30 min left | none | no | yes | yes |
| T-15 | 15 min left | none | no | yes | yes |
| T-5 | 5 min left | 10% | yes | no | no |
| T-2 | 2 min left | 25% | yes | no | no |
| T-1 | 1 min left | 40% | yes | no | no |
| Lockout | 0 min left | 100% | — | no | no |

Nothing appears on screen at T-30 or T-15. The visual ramp starts at T-5, so
the early warnings stay in Notification Center rather than dimming your display
half an hour before you finish. Minutes remaining round **up**, so T-1 fires the
moment you enter the final sixty seconds.

The dim overlay is one borderless window per display at `.floating` level with
mouse events passing through, so you can keep working through it. The floating
countdown is a separate `.statusBar`-level pill, also click-through.

## Snoozing

The T-30 and T-15 notifications carry a **Snooze 1 min** action. Tapping it
adds one minute to `snoozeMinutesGrantedToday` and re-ticks. Later stages do
not offer it — late enough to be meaningful, early enough not to be trivially
abused.

## Extending

The "hold for extension" control is live only at T-30 and T-15, and only while
your weekly budget has a slot left. You hold it for **2 seconds**. See
[Extensions and overrides](/overrides).

## Retuning the thresholds

`WarningIntervals` holds the five thresholds and lives in your settings. The
default is 30 / 15 / 5 / 2 / 1. Settings exposes sliders.

Curfew normalizes the values on every read and every write, so you cannot
produce a non-monotone sequence by dragging sliders in an awkward order. Each
stage is first clamped to its own ceiling and then forced below the stage above
it:

| Stage | Allowed range |
| --- | --- |
| T-1 | 1–30 min |
| T-2 | 2–45 min |
| T-5 | 5–90 min |
| T-15 | 15–180 min |
| T-30 | 30–360 min |

After clamping, the ordering is re-enforced from the bottom up, so the
sequence is always strictly increasing from the T-1 slot to the T-30 slot. The
stage names keep their labels even when you retune them — a "T-30" configured
at 45 minutes is still called T-30 in the activity log and in
`curfew-ctl status`.

## What gets recorded

Each escalation writes a `warning_escalated` event to the activity log with the
stage label in `note` and the minutes remaining in `minutes_value`. See
[the activity log](/activity-log).

## Multiple Macs

When CloudKit sync is running, the `LockoutState` record carries the warning
stages that have already fired today. A Mac joining mid-escalation suppresses
a banner another Mac has already shown, so opening a laptop at T-5 does not
re-fire the T-30 alarm you already saw. Sync is disabled by feature flag in the
shipping build, so this path is inactive today.
