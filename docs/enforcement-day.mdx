---
title: How a day runs
description: The arc from the morning gate through the warnings to the lockout.
---

Curfew calls the whole arc **the Curfew Flow**: an ordered sequence of surfaces
as the day opens, runs down, and closes. The enforcement engine ticks once per
second and re-evaluates where you are.

```
SUNRISE           DAY                DUSK                SUNDOWN            NIGHT
morning intent →  working window  →  warnings T-30…T-1 →  lockout overlay →  optional
(full screen)     (normal use)       (dim + timer)       + evening retro    shutdown
```

## Sunrise — the morning gate

At the day's first transition into the working phase, Curfew raises a
full-screen **Daybreak** overlay on every display and asks a short, editable
set of prompts. It commands the screen the way the lockout does, because the
day genuinely starts there.

It is not enforcement. There is no key interception, no respawn, and no
shutdown coupling, and a visible **Skip** hands the Mac back immediately. Each
gate prompts at most once per day; saving or skipping marks it resolved, and
the resolution survives a relaunch.

## Day — the working window

Normal use. The engine tracks wall-clock time toward `lockMinutes` and, in
hours or combined mode, active minutes toward `hoursLimitMinutes`. Nothing is
drawn on screen.

## Dusk — the warnings

From T-30 down, Curfew escalates through five stages: notifications first, then
a deepening dim overlay from T-5, then a floating countdown. You can snooze at
T-30 and T-15, and you can spend an extension at those same two stages. The
full table is in [Warnings and the countdown](/warnings).

## Sundown — the lockout

At T-0 the full-screen lockout takes over every display at `.screenSaver`
level, above the Dock and above any full-screen app. It captures mouse input,
intercepts the escape-hatch keyboard shortcuts, and shows the time, an
encouragement message, the unlock time, and the "Convince Me" override flow.
See [The lockout](/lockout).

When the evening reflection gate is enabled and still unresolved, it is
presented *inside* the lockout screen as an optional closing ritual. It is
non-blocking: saving or skipping it never delays or weakens enforcement, and
the clock and the override flow stay live underneath it.

## Night — optional shutdown

If you enabled auto-shutdown, Curfew waits out the configured delay, asks every
other app to quit gracefully, and then tells the Mac to shut down. Lockout
stays active whether or not the shutdown succeeds. See
[auto-shutdown](/lockout#auto-shutdown).

## The engine underneath

`CurfewEnforcementEngine` is a pure function. Given a schedule, a moment, the
extension minutes already granted today, an override end date, the warning
thresholds, and the minutes worked today, it returns a `CurfewEvaluation`:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `phase` | `working`, `warning`, `locked`, or `dayOff` |
| `warningStage` | `none`, `T-30`, `T-15`, `T-5`, `T-2`, `T-1`, or `lockout` |
| `minutesRemaining` | Minutes until the lock. `0` while locked, `Int.max` on a day off |
| `canRequestExtension` | True only at T-30 and T-15 with budget left |
| `lockDate` / `unlockDate` | Absolute dates, or `nil` on a day off |
| `trigger` | `time` or `hours` — which clock is driving the countdown |

The engine has no stored state and no side effects, so the same inputs always
give the same evaluation. Everything downstream — the overlay coordinator, the
notification manager, the menu bar item, the widget snapshot — reads that one
value.

## During an override

An active override keeps the phase at `working` past the nominal lock time, but
the warning stages **keep escalating underneath it**. You still see your real
deadline approaching while the override is in effect, rather than having the
countdown reset. When the override expires, the engine locks immediately if the
window has passed.

## Days off

A day marked `isDayOff` produces no schedule window, and the engine returns the
prebuilt `dayOff` evaluation. No warnings fire, no overlay is drawn, and
nothing shuts down.
