title: Schedule and curfew modes
description: Per-day lock and unlock times, days off, hours limits, and presets.
---

A Curfew schedule is a `WeeklySchedule`: one `DayRule` per weekday. The engine
resolves that into concrete dates once per second and decides what phase you
are in.

## `DayRule`

Each day carries five fields.

<ParamField path="isDayOff" type="Bool">
  When true, no enforcement window exists for that day. The lock and unlock
  times are still stored, so turning the day back on restores your previous
  values instead of resetting them.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="lockMinutes" type="Int">
  Minutes past midnight at which the Mac locks. `1080` is 18:00.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="unlockMinutes" type="Int">
  Minutes past midnight at which the Mac unlocks. Any value less than or equal
  to `lockMinutes` is read as the following calendar day, so `480` with a lock
  at `1080` means 08:00 tomorrow.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="mode" type="CurfewMode" default="time">
  How the day's curfew triggers. See [Curfew modes](#curfew-modes).
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="hoursLimitMinutes" type="Int?">
  In `hours` or `combined` mode, lock after this many minutes of active work.
  `nil` in `time` mode. Typical values are `480` (8 hours) and `600`
  (10 hours).
</ParamField>

Times are stored as minutes past midnight rather than as `Date` values so the
schedule itself is timezone-independent. `WeeklySchedule.scheduleWindow` turns
them into absolute dates at evaluation time using the device calendar, which is
what makes daylight-saving transitions behave.

<Note>
`DayRule` also has an `exception` field. It is a `Codable` placeholder with no
contents today and is always `nil`. It exists so per-day holiday overrides can
land later without a schema migration.
</Note>

### Overnight windows

Because `unlockMinutes <= lockMinutes` means "next day", a schedule can run
past midnight. `scheduleWindow` also checks the *previous* day's rule first: if
you are inside yesterday's lock-to-unlock span, that window is the one that
applies. A 02:00 curfew with an 08:00 unlock therefore keeps enforcing at
03:00, rather than falling into a gap.

## Curfew modes

`CurfewMode` has three cases.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="time — wall clock (the default)">
    The lock fires at `lockMinutes`. This is the v0.1 behavior and the default
    for every rule, including rules decoded from older saved settings.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="hours — accumulated work">
    The lock fires once you have accumulated `hoursLimitMinutes` of active
    work today. The effective lock time is recomputed each tick as
    `now + (hoursLimit + extensions − worked)`. Idle time is excluded before
    the engine sees the number; `IdleWatcher` uses a five-minute cutoff.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="combined — whichever comes first">
    Both deadlines are computed and the nearer one wins. The evaluation carries
    a `trigger` of `time` or `hours` so the lockout screen and the MCP tools
    can say which clock fired.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Extensions apply to **both** clocks. Asking for fifteen more minutes pushes the
wall-clock deadline out by fifteen minutes and raises the hours budget by
fifteen minutes, because "fifteen more minutes tonight" should mean the same
thing in every mode.

## Presets

`SchedulePreset` names three ready-made weekly schedules. All three mark
Saturday and Sunday as days off.

| Preset | Label in Settings | Lock | Unlock |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `nineToFive` | 9-to-5 | 18:00 | 08:00 |
| `startupHours` | Startup Hours | 20:00 | 08:00 |
| `halfDay` | Half Day | 13:00 | 08:00 |

Applying a preset is not a shortcut around the anti-bypass policy. It queues a
schedule change like any other edit and is classified the same way.

## Changing the schedule

`SchedulePolicyEngine` compares your proposed schedule against the active one,
day by day, and classifies the result.

| Classification | What triggers it | When it takes effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `weaker` | Later lock, earlier unlock, a day turned off, any mode change, or a higher hours limit — on any single day | 24 hours after you request it |
| `stricter` | Only tightening changes: earlier lock, later unlock, a day turned on, or a lower hours limit | The next calendar midnight |
| `no_change` | Every day is identical | Immediately, and nothing is queued |

A single loosening day makes the whole change `weaker`, even if six other days
got stricter. That is the point: the policy exists so you cannot escape
tonight's session by editing Settings at 17:55.

Mode changes are deliberately conservative. Any transition between `time`,
`hours`, and `combined` classifies as `weaker`, even when the new mode is
objectively stricter, because the real strictness depends on how many minutes
you have already worked and that cannot be evaluated at classification time.
Waiting out 24 hours for a genuine tightening is the price of never letting a
mode flip slip past the gate.

A queued change lives in `pendingScheduleChange` with its `requestedAt`,
`effectiveAt`, and classification. Both the schedule view and
`curfew-ctl schedule` show the pending effective date. An assistant using
[`curfew_set_schedule`](/mcp-tools#curfew-set-schedule) goes through exactly
the same classifier.

## Reading the schedule

From the shell:

```bash
curfew-ctl schedule
curfew-ctl schedule --json
```

From an assistant, [`curfew_schedule`](/mcp-tools#curfew-schedule) returns the
same seven rules plus any pending change.
